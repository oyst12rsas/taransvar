STUDENT PROJECTS • MASTER'S THESES • RESEARCH

Help build, test and challenge a new Internet security model.

TaraSec is no longer just a firewall project. Firewalls, honeypots and other security tools can act as sensors in a wider collaborative network, while identity, distributed systems, AI, software, policy and business models determine whether the system can work at scale.

Many systems observe activity → Networks provide accountable unit context → AI and software correlate evidence → People, policy and organisations decide how to act

A WORKING PLATFORM

Start with something that already exists.

The experimental environment already includes NetBird and WireGuard networks, Linux VMs and gateways, C/Perl/PHP/database components, firewall and honeypot telemetry, a unit-identity model, AI assessment work and operational web infrastructure.

Students can improve the implementation, design replacement components, measure it, attack it, study adoption, challenge the assumptions, or propose a better architecture. A finding that part of TaraSec does not work is a useful result.

MANY DISCIPLINES

TaraSec needs more than cybersecurity specialists.

01

AI & data science

Threat assessment, confidence, evidence correlation, explainability, model evaluation, false-positive control, adversarial inputs and human-in-the-loop decision systems.

02

Software & system development

Architecture, APIs, databases, services, installers, observability, testing, distributed state, reliability, user interfaces and migration from prototype code to production-quality systems.

03

Networking & Internet infrastructure

Routing, gateways, IPv4/IPv6, identity across NAT and mobility, signalling, protocol design, performance, interoperability and deployment through ISPs or enterprise networks.

04

Cybersecurity

Threat modelling, sensors, honeypots, firewall integration, malicious-report resistance, compromised participants, authentication, cryptography and end-to-end security testing.

05

Business & management

How do you create incentives for ISPs, vendors, enterprises and governments to participate? Study business models, rollout strategy, governance, partnerships, network effects, market barriers and sustainable organisation.

06

Privacy, law & governance

Personal-data boundaries, pseudonymous unit identifiers, accountability, auditability, roles between network owners and TaraSec, regulatory implications and governance of a shared security network.

07

UX, communication & adoption

Design operator workflows, explain AI decisions, make warnings actionable, document the system and study how technical participants understand and trust collaborative security information.

08

Economics & public policy

Model whether collaborative attribution changes the economics of cybercrime, assess externalities and incentives, and study possible public-sector or critical-infrastructure adoption.

EXAMPLE PROJECTS

Choose a real unresolved problem.

AI assessment pipeline

Build and evaluate a system that combines unit identity, event history and multiple sensor observations into evidence, confidence, recommendations and auditable human decisions.

Global unit identity architecture

Design how network owners can publish stable technical unit identifiers without exposing customer identity, while surviving address changes, NAT, roaming and multiple network layers.

Collaborative signalling protocol

Design and compare secure in-band and out-of-band mechanisms for distributing unit-level risk information across independent networks.

Production system redesign

Review the existing C, Perl, PHP and database system and propose or implement a modern modular architecture with testing, APIs, observability and controlled deployment.

Sensor ecosystem

Turn firewalls, honeypots, IDS/IPS products and other systems into interoperable sensors that produce trustworthy, normalized observations rather than isolated alerts.

Business model and global rollout

Investigate who gains value, who bears cost, how participation can reach critical mass, what commercial/non-profit structures fit, and how TaraSec could expand from pilots to a global network.

Governance and accountability

Design rules for reporting, appeals, malicious participants, audit trails, privacy responsibilities, AI oversight and distributed authority between TaraSec and participating organisations.

Build, attack and measure the network

Deploy realistic test scenarios, introduce malicious nodes and failures, measure overhead and latency, and establish where the architecture scales or breaks.

BRING YOUR OWN QUESTION

The project does not have to be on this page.
